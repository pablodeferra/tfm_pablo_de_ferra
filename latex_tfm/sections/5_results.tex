\section{RESULTS}
\label{sec:results}

In this section we present the results of the foreground power spectrum analysis described in Section~\ref{sec:methodology}. The analysis is performed over the multipole range $20 \leq \ell \leq 200$ using the combined 14-band of QUIJOTE, WMAP and Planck dataset. Two complementary fitting approaches are applied: a global power-law fit described in Section~\ref{sec:fitting_methods_pl}, and an independent bin-to-bin fit described in Section~\ref{sec:fitting_methods_btb}. For each approach, results are obtained for three Galactic masks defined by latitude cuts at $|b| > 10^\circ$, $15^\circ$, and $20^\circ$ (Section~\ref{sec:masks}), allowing us to assess the robustness of the derived foreground parameters with respect to Galactic plane contamination. In all cases, the EE and BB polarization modes are fitted separately.

\paragraph{}

The foreground model and the Bayesian inference framework are described in Sections~\ref{sec:foreground_model} and \ref{sec:mcmc}, respectively. This analysis follows approaches similar to those of \citet{Planck_2018XI}, \citet{Choi_2015}, \citet{Hensley_2022}, and \citet{Martire_2022}. The baseline results presented here include both auto- and cross-spectra (105 spectra in total). A complementary analysis restricted to cross-spectra only, which avoids any noise bias contribution from the auto-spectra, is presented in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_1}. 

\paragraph{}

The multipole range adopted here starts at $\ell = 20$, rather than the $\ell \geq 30$ range used in several comparable studies \citep{QUIJOTE_IV, Martire_2022}. The choice is motivated by the behaviour of the BB-mode power-law fit, as the BB signal is intrinsically fainter than EE and falls steeply with multipole, the first bin ($\ell_{\rm eff} = 29$) carries a large fraction of the constraining power on the angular spectral index $\alpha_s$. When this bin is excluded, the remaining bins at smaller angular scales provide insufficient leverage to anchor the power-law slope, leading to steeper and poorly constrained values of $\alpha_s$. Including the first bin resolves this degeneracy and yields well-constrained, physically consistent results for the BB mode. This effect is demonstrated in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_2}, where results obtained over the range $30 \leq \ell \leq 200$ are presented for comparison.

\subsection{Power-law fitting}
\label{sec:results_pl}
% --------------------------------

The global power-law model (Section~\ref{sec:fitting_methods_pl}) is fitted simultaneously over all nine multipole bins and all 105 frequency spectra. The posterior constraints on the synchrotron amplitude $A_s$, multipole spectral index $\alpha_s$, frequency spectral index $\beta_s$, dust amplitude $A_d$, multipole spectral index $\alpha_d$, frequency spectral index $\beta_d$, and synchrotron-dust correlation coefficient $\rho$ are reported as the median and $68\%$ credible intervals. In order to quantify the goodness of the fit, in Appendix \ref{sec:appendix_3}, we present a table including all the reduced $\chi^2$ values obtained for all power-law fittings of this work.

\paragraph{}

Results are obtained separately for EE and BB polarization modes, and for two dataset combinations: WMAP+Planck (WP+Pl) and QUIJOTE+WMAP+Planck (QJ+WP+Pl). These combinations are used to quantify the impact of including QUĲOTE-MFI data in the analysis.

The global power-law model provides a reasonable first-order description of the measured spectra over the fitted multipole range, as illustrated in Figures~\ref{fig:synch_fit} and \ref{fig:dust_fit}. In particular, the cross-spectra are generally well reproduced in both the synchrotron- and dust-dominated bands. For the auto-spectra, individual multipole bins occasionally deviate from the best-fit curve by more than $1\sigma$, most noticeably at intermediate multipoles where the residual noise contributions differ between band pairs. These deviations are more pronounced for auto- than for cross-spectra, consistent with the fact that auto-spectra include an additional noise-bias term whose estimation and subtraction can introduce extra scatter. Overall, the fit captures the main frequency and multipole dependences of the data, but the reduced $\chi^2$ values reported in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_3} show that the agreement is not uniformly good across masks and modes. When the fit is restricted to cross-spectra only (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_1}), the $\chi^2$ values improve, supporting the interpretation that residual mismodelling of the auto-spectrum noise bias contributes to this scatter.

\subsubsection{Synchrotron amplitude $A_s$}
\label{sec:synch_amp_pl}

The synchrotron amplitude $A_s$ is defined as the value of the power spectrum $C_\ell^{\rm sync}$ at the reference multipole $\ell_{\rm ref} = 80$ and at the reference frequency $\nu_{\rm ref} = 23\,\mathrm{GHz}$ (the WMAP K-band channel), as given by Equation \ref{eq:synch_model}. It is presented in Table \ref{tab:fit_all_masks_all_pairs_ell20-200} in units of $10^{-3}\,\mu\mathrm{K}^2$ and is constrained separately for the EE and BB polarization modes.

The EE amplitude is consistently larger than the BB amplitude across all three masks and both dataset combinations, reflecting the well-established dominance of the E-mode over the B-mode in synchrotron polarization \citep{Krachmalnicoff_2018, Martire_2022}. For the baseline QJ+WP+Pl dataset, the EE amplitude ranges from $A_s^{EE} \approx 6.7 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mu\mathrm{K}^2$ for the $|b| > 10^\circ$ mask to $A_s^{EE} \approx 5.9 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mu\mathrm{K}^2$ for the $|b| > 20^\circ$ mask, reflecting the decreasing contribution of low-latitude emission as the Galactic plane emission is masked. 

The synchrotron B-to-E ratio $r \equiv A_s^{BB}/A_s^{EE}$ provides a direct measure of the relative power in the two polarization modes. For the WMAP and Planck dataset we find $r = 0.283 \pm 0.052$, $0.319 \pm 0.071$, and $0.241 \pm 0.062$ for the $|b| > 10^\circ$, $15^\circ$, and $20^\circ$ masks, respectively. Adding QUIJOTE-MFI data tightens the constraints considerably and yields $r = 0.224 \pm 0.026$, $0.228 \pm 0.033$, and $0.229 \pm 0.036$ for the same three masks. The three values are mutually consistent within $1\sigma$, indicating that the ratio is stable with respect to the choice of Galactic cut. The QJ+WP+Pl estimates are also in good agreement with the values $0.22 \pm 0.02$ reported by \citet{Martire_2022} using WMAP and Planck data, and with the value $0.26 \pm 0.08$ obtained by \citet{QUIJOTE_IV} using the QUIJOTE-MFI 11~GHz map over the range $30 < \ell < 200$ and the same $10^\circ$ and $20^\circ$ masks as we use in this work. These ratios are significantly lower than the values of $\sim 0.5$ found by \citet{Krachmalnicoff_2018} using S-PASS data at 2.3~GHz, a discrepancy commonly attributed to Faraday rotation of the polarization angle at low frequencies and mid-Galactic latitudes \citep{Fuskeland_2021}. Compared to thermal dust, for which the B-to-E ratio is typically $\sim 0.5$ \citep{Planck_2018XI}, synchrotron emission shows a markedly lower and more latitude-stable ratio of $\sim 0.2$.

\paragraph{}

The inclusion of the QUIJOTE-MFI data leads to a significant reduction in the uncertainty on $A_s$ for both modes. For EE, the $1\sigma$ uncertainty is reduced by approximately a factor of 1.6 for all masks. For the BB mode, where the synchrotron signal is fainter and the constraints from WMAP and Planck alone are limited, the improvement is similarly notable, with uncertainties reduced by a factor of 1.8. This shows the key role of QUIJOTE-MFI in anchoring the low-frequency synchrotron amplitude, particularly for the BB mode.

\subsubsection{Synchrotron angular spectral index $\alpha_s$}
\label{sec:synch_alp_pl}

The angular spectral index $\alpha_s$ parametrizes the multipole-dependence of the synchrotron power spectrum as a power law about the pivot $\ell_{\rm ref} = 80$ (Section~\ref{sec:synchrotron}). Negative values indicate that the power falls with increasing $\ell$, as expected for diffuse Galactic emission dominated by large angular scales.

For the QJ+WP+Pl dataset, the EE spectral index takes values of $\alpha_s^{EE} \approx -3.7$ across all three masks, indicating a steep, large-scale-dominated spectrum. The BB spectral index is less tightly constrained, yielding $\alpha_s^{BB} \approx -3.3$ to $-3.4$ with uncertainties of $\sim 0.12\text{-}0.16$, also consistent across masks. The QUIJOTE-MFI data reduces the uncertainty on $\alpha_s^{EE}$ by roughly a factor of $\sim 1.7$ and on $\alpha_s^{BB}$ by a factor of $\sim 1.5$ with respect to WP+Pl alone, primarily by breaking the degeneracy between the angular and frequency dependences of the synchrotron model.

Although the BB values are nominally less steep than EE by $ \Delta \alpha \approx 0.3\text{-}0.4$ across all masks and dataset combinations, the difference is only significant at about the $\sim 2\sigma$ level because the BB uncertainties are much larger. For that reason, the current data do not provide robust evidence for a genuine difference between the EE and BB angular slopes. This becomes even clearer when the baseline fit is compared with the $30 \leq \ell \leq 200$ case discussed in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_2}. Once the first multipole bin ($\ell_{\rm eff} = 29$) is removed, the trend reverses and the BB slope becomes steeper than the EE slope for all three masks (e.g., $\alpha_s^{BB} \approx -4.1$ versus $\alpha_s^{EE} \approx -2.9$ for $|b| > 10^\circ$), but with very large BB uncertainties. The fact that the sign of the $\alpha_s^{EE} - \alpha_s^{BB}$ difference changes with the fitting range shows that the relative slope is not yet robustly constrained and remains sensitive to the low-$\ell$ binning.

This same dependence on the fitted multipole range also helps explain the comparison with previous works. The baseline EE values found here ($\alpha_s^{EE} \approx -3.6$ to $-3.8$) are steeper than the $-2.95 \pm 0.07$ reported by \citet{Martire_2022}, who used WMAP and Planck data over a sky fraction of $0.4$ and a fitting interval of $30 \leq \ell \leq 300$. Our results are also steeper than the $-3.16 \pm 0.03$ obtained by \citet{Krachmalnicoff_2018} using S-PASS at 2.3~GHz over $20 \leq \ell \leq 140$ and a sky fraction of $0.22$. However, in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_2}, when the first bin is excluded, the EE slopes flatten to $\alpha_s^{EE} \approx -2.7$ to $-3.0$, bringing them much closer to the reference values. This suggests that part of the discrepancy is driven by the extra leverage introduced by the $\ell_{\rm eff} = 29$ bin in our baseline analysis, while any remaining disagreement with S-PASS may also reflect the different sky regions covered by the two experiments. Notably, \citet{Krachmalnicoff_2018} also reported steep EE slopes when the $\ell_{\rm eff} = 29$ bin was included, obtaining $\alpha_s^{EE} = -3.32 \pm 0.07$ and a flatter $\alpha_s^{BB} = -3.03 \pm 0.12$ for a $|b| > 40^{\circ}$ mask, a pattern consistent with the behaviour found in the present work.


To further investigate the sensitivity of $\alpha_s$ to the binning choice, we performed a set of auxiliary single-band fits using the QUIJOTE 11~GHz, WMAP 23~GHz, and Planck 30~GHz maps independently. In these tests, each band is fitted to a simple power law in multipole space with only amplitude $A$ and slope $\alpha$ as free parameters (i.e., without the frequency spectral index $\beta_s$, since no frequency combination is involved).

Two model variants were considered. In the first case, the data were fitted with a pure power-law model. In the second case, an additional constant term (independent of the multipole) was included in the model to account for possible mismodelings of the noise and contributions from point radio sources. We performed these tests using two different binning schemes, these are: $\Delta\ell = 10$ and $\Delta\ell = 20$.


With $\Delta\ell = 10$ binning, the slope estimates remain broadly consistent with $\alpha_s \approx -3$ for both polarization modes across all three bands, in good agreement with the global power-law results obtained in \cite{QUIJOTE_IV} and \cite{Martire_2022}. With $\Delta\ell = 20$ binning the fitting is more sensitive to whether the lowest-$\ell$ bin is included. When the first bin is excluded ($\ell_{\rm eff} = 29$), the EE slope tends toward flatter values near $-3$, while the BB mode is too faint to be well constrained, yielding large uncertainties and unreliable slope estimates, particularly for WMAP and Planck, explaining the results presented in Appendix \ref{sec:appendix_2} for the multipole spectral index. When the first bin is included, the EE slope steepens, consistent with what is seen in Table~\ref{tab:fit_all_masks_all_pairs_ell20-200}, but the BB fit improves notably as the high-signal first bin provides the leverage needed to anchor the power-law slope, leading to well-constrained values of $\alpha_s^{BB}$ near $-3$. This confirms that the steeper EE slopes in the baseline analysis are a consequence of the additional low-$\ell$ leverage, and that including this bin is essential for obtaining reliable BB constraints with the current data and binning scheme.

In this context, it is useful to consider the reduced $\chi^2$ values reported in Table~\ref{tab:chi2_all} (Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_3}). For the global power-law fit, the $\chi^2_{\rm red}$ values are in several cases significantly above unity, particularly for the EE mode at low Galactic latitude cuts, suggesting that the true multipole dependence of the synchrotron power spectrum is not perfectly captured by a single power law over the range $20 \leq \ell \leq 200$. In contrast, the bin-to-bin results (Section~\ref{sec:results_btb}) yield $\chi^2_{\rm red}$ values close to unity for most bins and masks, confirming that the model is flexible enough to describe the data at each individual scale. This discrepancy between the global and per-bin goodness of fit implies that the angular spectral index $\alpha_s$ derived from the power-law fit should not be over-interpreted, as it represents an effective average slope over the full multipole range, but the underlying spectrum may deviate from a strict power law, and its precise value is consequently sensitive to the fitting range and binning choice.

\subsubsection{Synchrotron frequency spectral index $\beta_s$}
\label{sec:synch_bet_pl}

The frequency spectral index $\beta_s$ governs the power-law scaling of the synchrotron SED in Rayleigh-Jeans brightness temperature units, $T_{\rm RJ} \propto \nu^{\beta_s}$, and is directly related to the energy distribution of the cosmic-ray electrons responsible for synchrotron radiation \citep{Rybicki_1979, Padovani_2021}. Of all the parameters in the foreground model, $\beta_s$ is the one most directly sensitive to the frequency coverage of the dataset, because its constraint comes almost entirely from the lever arm between the lowest and highest frequency channels at which synchrotron emission is detectable. This makes $\beta_s$ the parameter for which the inclusion of the QUIJOTE-MFI bands produces the most notable improvement.

\paragraph{}

Without QUIJOTE, the WP+Pl dataset yields EE spectral indices in the range $\beta_s^{EE} \approx -3.0$ to $-3.1$ across the three masks, with uncertainties of $0.04$-$0.06$.The corresponding BB estimates are nominally steeper by $\sim 0.1$-$0.3$, but consistent with the EE values within $1$-$2\sigma$ given BB uncertainties of $\sim 0.14$-$0.19$. These relatively large errors reflect the limited frequency baseline of WMAP and Planck alone, for which the synchrotron signal is not sufficiently strong or well separated from other components to tightly constrain the spectral index.

Adding the four QUIJOTE-MFI bands extends the synchrotron baseline down to 11~GHz, making a significant improvement in the fitting. For the QJ+WP+Pl combination the EE uncertainties decrease by a factor of $2.6$-$2.9$ depending on mask, and the BB uncertainties by a factor of $2.8$-$3.0$, making $\beta_s$ the parameter with the largest error reduction brought by QUIJOTE. The resulting QJ+WP+Pl posteriors converge tightly around $\beta_s \approx -3.0$ to $-3.1$ for both polarization modes, with EE errors of $\sim 0.02$ and BB errors of order $\sim0.06$. Across all three masks, the EE and BB estimates are mutually consistent within $\sim 2\sigma$, providing no statistically significant evidence for a difference between the two polarization modes.

\paragraph{}



These values are in excellent agreement with the canonical synchrotron spectral index, $\beta_s \approx -3.1$, reported in previous studies. \cite{Fuskeland_2014} found $\beta_s = -3.12 \pm 0.04$ from an analysis of WMAP polarization data, while \citet{Martire_2022} obtained $\beta_s^{EE} = -3.00 \pm 0.10$ and $\beta_s^{BB} = -3.05 \pm 0.36$ using a similar multi-frequency power spectrum approach combining WMAP and Planck data. Our QJ+WP+Pl EE estimates ($\beta_s^{EE} \approx -3.08$ to $-3.11$) are consistent with both studies within $1\sigma$.

For the BB-mode spectral indices, we obtain $\beta_s^{BB} \approx -2.98$ to $-3.02$. These values are compatible with the results of \citet{Martire_2022}, while providing a clear improvement in the constraints on this parameter for the BB polarization mode. Furthermore, \citet{Fuskeland_2021} measured a steeper value, $\beta_s = -3.22 \pm 0.08$, using Faraday-corrected S-PASS data combined with WMAP. This measurement is also consistent with our results at the $\sim 2\sigma$ level.



\subsubsection{Synchrotron-dust spatial correlation $\rho$}
\label{sec:rho_pl}

The correlation coefficient $\rho$ quantifies the degree of spatial correlation between the synchrotron and thermal dust polarized emission along the line of sight (Section~\ref{sec:corr}; \citealp{Choi_2015}). A positive $\rho$ indicates that the two foreground components share a common polarization structure, which is physically expected when the GMF aligns the polarization directions of both components over large angular scales \citep{Choi_2015}. This parameter is constrained jointly with the other foreground parameters in the global power-law fit and is bounded by $\rho \in [-1, 1]$.

For both dataset combinations and all three masks, we recover small but significantly non-zero positive values of $\rho$, confirming a weak spatial correlation between the two foreground components. For the QJ+WP+Pl dataset, the EE correlation coefficient decreases with increasing Galactic latitude cut, from $\rho^{EE} = 0.096 \pm 0.005$ for the $|b| > 10^\circ$ mask to $0.023 \pm 0.007$ for the $|b| > 20^\circ$ mask. This trend is consistent with the expectation that synchrotron and dust emission are both stronger at low latitudes. As the mask becomes more restrictive and the retained sky is dominated by high-latitude regions with less coherent magnetic field structure, the spatial correlation between the two components decreases. These values are in concordance with the findings of \citet{Choi_2015}, who reported $\rho \sim 0.2$ from a bin-to-bin analysis of WMAP 9-year data.

The BB correlation coefficient shows a different behaviour, with $\rho^{BB}$ remains relatively stable across masks, taking values of $\sim 0.11$-$0.12$ for QJ+WP+Pl, and does not exhibit the pronounced decline observed for EE. \citet{Choi_2015} similarly found that BB values of $\rho$ are generally comparable to the corresponding EE values and do not show a significant decrease with sky fraction. This behaviour is consistent with the distinct physical origin of the two polarization modes in the presence of the GMF. E-mode emission is primarily generated by the ordered, large-scale component of the GMF, which is strongest near the Galactic plane and decreases toward high Galactic latitudes. In contrast, B-mode emission traces the turbulent component of the field \citep{Planck_2016XXX, Planck_2015XIX}. Because the turbulent magnetic field is present at all Galactic latitudes, the B-mode correlation between synchrotron and dust emission is expected to be more spatially uniform than the E-mode correlation, as obtained in this work.

The EE-BB difference in $\rho$ grows with the Galactic cut. For the $|b| > 10^\circ$ mask the two modes are consistent at the $\sim 1.3\sigma$ level, but the discrepancy increases to $\sim 3.4\sigma$ for $|b| > 15^\circ$ and reaches $\sim 5.9\sigma$ for $|b| > 20^\circ$, providing clear evidence that the two polarization modes trace spatially distinct correlation structures at high Galactic latitudes.

\paragraph{}

The overall magnitude and mode-dependent behaviour of $\rho$ are consistent with previous studies. \citet{Planck_2018XI} also reported low but positive correlations in their joint synchrotron-dust power spectrum analysis, finding $\rho$ values consistent with ours at the $\sim 0.1$ level. \citet{Krachmalnicoff_2018}, using S-PASS at 2.3~GHz combined with Planck 353~GHz data, found per-bin values of $\rho$ that are generally positive and range from near zero up to $\sim 0.40$ in the most inclusive masks at the lowest multipoles, but that cluster around $0.1$-$0.2$ across most bins, multipoles, and sky fractions for both polarization modes. This behaviour is in good agreement with our global estimates of $\rho \sim 0.1$-$0.12$, given that these values represent averages over the full multipole range. Our results confirm that the synchrotron-dust correlation, while non-negligible, remains at the level of $\rho \sim 0.1$ or below for most of the sky outside the Galactic plane, and exhibits different behaviour in the E- and B-modes.

\subsubsection{Thermal dust parameters}
\label{sec:dust_pl}

The thermal dust component is parametrized by the amplitude $A_d$ at $\ell_{\rm ref} = 80$ and $\nu_0 = 353\,\mathrm{GHz}$, the angular spectral index $\alpha_d$, and the modified-blackbody frequency spectral index $\beta_d$ (Section~\ref{sec:dust}). Because the dust signal is anchored by the Planck HFI bands (100-353~GHz), which are present in both dataset combinations, the inclusion of QUIJOTE data has virtually no effect on the dust parameters. The WP+Pl and QJ+WP+Pl posteriors are identical for all three dust parameters. This is expected, since the QUIJOTE bands at 11 and 17~GHz carry negligible dust emission, and confirms that the synchrotron and dust parameters are well separated by the fit.

\paragraph{}

The EE dust amplitude decreases monotonically from $A_d^{EE} \approx 3.2 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mu\mathrm{K}^2$ for the $|b| > 10^\circ$ mask to $A_d^{EE} \approx 1.4 \cdot 10^{-3}\,\mu\mathrm{K}^2$ for the $|b| > 20^\circ$ mask, reflecting the decreasing density of dust-emitting material at higher Galactic latitudes. The BB amplitude follows the same trend, decreasing from $A_d^{BB} \approx 2.4 \cdot 10^{-3}$ to $0.9 \cdot 10^{-3}\, \mu\mathrm{K}^2$, and is consistently smaller than EE. The dust B-to-E ratio $A_d^{BB}/A_d^{EE}$ ranges from $\sim 0.73$ ($|b| > 10^\circ$) to $\sim 0.64$ ($|b| > 20^\circ$), in broad agreement with the ratio of $\sim 0.5$-$0.6$ found by \citet{Planck_2018XI} and \citet{Planck_2016XXX}. Our values are slightly higher, which may reflect the different multipole ranges and sky fractions used. 

\paragraph{}

The EE angular index is $\alpha_d^{EE} \approx -2.5$ for all three masks, with the $|b| > 20^\circ$ mask yielding a slightly flatter value of $-2.478 \pm 0.008$. The BB values are consistently less steep, with $\alpha_d^{BB} \approx -2.2$ to $-2.3$, indicating that the dust BB power spectrum falls off less steeply with multipole than EE. This EE-BB difference is highly significant (above $5\sigma$ for all masks) and constitutes one of the most robust results of the fit. It is in good agreement with the findings of \citet{Planck_2018XI}, who reported $\alpha_d^{EE} = -2.42 \pm 0.02$ and $\alpha_d^{BB} = -2.54 \pm 0.02$ over a similar multipole range, though they found BB to be marginally steeper than EE, the opposite of the trend seen here. \citet{Planck_2016XXX} reported for both EE-and BB-modes $\alpha_d \approx -2.42 \pm 0.02$ for high-latitude sky at 353~GHz over $40 < \ell < 600$. Our EE values are close to this estimate, while the BB values are slightly flatter, which may reflect the different multipole ranges and sky fractions used in the two analyses.

\paragraph{}

The MBB spectral index $\beta_d$ is remarkably stable across all masks, dataset combinations, and polarization modes, with values clustering tightly around $\beta_d \approx 1.53$. The EE estimates range from $1.486 \pm 0.008$ ($|b| > 20^\circ$) to $1.552 \pm 0.007$ ($|b| > 15^\circ$), while the BB values span $1.530 \pm 0.005$ to $1.533 \pm 0.009$. The EE and BB values are mutually consistent within $\sim 1\sigma$ for all masks except $|b| > 20^\circ$, where the EE value is slightly lower. These values are in excellent agreement with the Planck measurements of $\beta_d = 1.53 \pm 0.02$ from polarization data \citep{Planck_2018XI} and with the intensity-based estimates of $\beta_d \approx 1.51$-$1.59$ from \citet{Planck_XXII}. The tight consistency of $\beta_d$ across masks and modes confirms that the dust SED is well characterized by a single modified blackbody with $T_d = 19.6\,\mathrm{K}$ over the sky fractions and multipole range considered in this work.

\subsection{Bin-to-bin fitting}
\label{sec:results_btb}
% --------------------------------

In the bin-to-bin approach (see Section~\ref{sec:fitting_methods_btb}), each of the nine multipole bins is fitted independently, without imposing any global power-law dependence on $\ell$. The per-bin parameter vector is $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\rm bin} = \{A_s, \beta_s, A_d, \beta_d, \rho\}$, and results are obtained separately for EE and BB modes. This approach allows the identification of scale-dependent trends and potential deviations from the global power-law behaviour found in Section~\ref{sec:results_pl}. The Gaussian prior $\beta_s \sim \mathcal{N}(-3.1, 0.3^2)$ (Section~\ref{sec:prior}) is applied to all bins, but only becomes relevant in those where the low-frequency signal-to-noise ratio is insufficient to independently constrain the synchrotron SED, in the remaining bins the data drive the posterior well within the prior width. This prior is anchored by the global power-law constraints derived in Section~\ref{sec:synch_bet_pl}, where the QJ+WP+Pl posteriors yield $\beta_s$ uncertainties of $\sim 0.02$ for EE and $\sim 0.06$ for BB, being an order of magnitude narrower than the prior width of $0.3$, confirming that the posterior in well-constrained bins is dominated by the data rather than the prior.

\paragraph{}

In this section we will account all auto- and cross-spectra of the QUIJOTE, WMAP and Planck dataset for the fit. Posterior constraints (median and $68\%$ credible intervals) on $A_s$, $\beta_s$, $A_d$, $\beta_d$, and $\rho$ are reported as a function of the effective multipole $\ell_{\rm eff}$ for each bin, including the reduced $\chi^2$ values obtained for each bin of the fitting. The full numerical results for all three masks are listed in Tables~\ref{tab:bin_to_bin_galcut10_all_pairs}, \ref{tab:bin_to_bin_galcut15_all_pairs} and \ref{tab:bin_to_bin_galcut20_all_pairs}. The corresponding cross-spectra-only results are provided in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_1}.


\subsubsection{Amplitude ratios}

Figure~\ref{fig:amp_ratio_bin-to-bin} shows the B-to-E amplitude ratios $A_s^{BB}/A_s^{EE}$ and $A_d^{BB}/A_d^{EE}$ as a function of $\ell_{\rm eff}$ for each mask. The synchrotron ratio scatters around the reference line $A_s^{BB}/A_s^{EE} = 0.2$ with no systematic trend with scale or mask, in good agreement with the global estimates of $\sim 0.22$-$0.23$ (Section~\ref{sec:synch_amp_pl}) and with the results of \citet{QUIJOTE_IV} and \citet{Martire_2022}. The larger scatter at high $\ell$ is expected due to the decreasing signal-to-noise ratio of the synchrotron B-mode amplitude in individual bins. 


The dust ratio is likewise approximately stable, clustering around $0.6$-$0.7$ and consistent with the global values of $\sim 0.64$-$0.73$, in agreement with results found by \citet{Planck_2018XI}, which show that at low multipoles the measured B-to-E power ratio is in the range of about $0.5$-$1$. However, these values are consistently higher than the $\sim 0.5$ reported by \citet{Choi_2015} and \citet{Planck_2016XXX}. Notably, Figure~\ref{fig:amp_ratio_bin-to-bin} shows a clear dependence on the Galactic mask, as the more restrictive cuts yield a lower dust ratio, suggesting that residual large-scale Galactic emission may bias the amplitude ratio upward in the most inclusive sky fractions.

\subsubsection{Synchrotron frequency spectral index $\beta_s$}

The per-bin $\beta_s$ posteriors are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:beta_s_bin-to-bin} and are broadly consistent with the global power-law estimates (Section~\ref{sec:synch_bet_pl}). For the well-constrained EE bins at low $\ell$, the values cluster around $\beta_s^{EE} \approx -3.09$ to $-3.24$ across all three masks, in good agreement with the global QJ+WP+Pl results of $\beta_s^{EE} \approx -3.08$ to $-3.11$. Individual bins at intermediate and high multipoles have uncertainties of order $0.2$-$0.5$, as the signal-to-noise ratio decreases and the Gaussian prior $\beta_s \sim \mathcal{N}(-3.1, 0.3^2)$ progressively governs the posterior. For BB, the first bin ($\ell_{\rm eff} = 29$) is well constrained with $\beta_s^{BB} \approx -3.03$ to $-3.10$ across masks, consistent with the global estimates. At higher multipoles the synchrotron B-mode signal is too faint to independently constrain the spectral index, and the posterior widens considerably. No significant scale dependence of $\beta_s$ is detected in either polarization mode. The scatter of the estimates around $-3.1$ is consistent with statistical noise and the effect of the prior in low signal-to-noise bins. These results confirm the findings of Section~\ref{sec:synch_bet_pl} and are consistent with \citet{Martire_2022}, as well as with \citet{Choi_2015} and \citet{Planck_2018XI}, who adopted a similar bin-to-bin approach including a Gaussian prior on the synchrotron spectral index.

\subsubsection{Dust spectral index $\beta_d$}

The per-bin $\beta_d$ posteriors are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:beta_d_bin-to-bin}. As expected from the dominance of the Planck HFI bands in constraining the dust SED, $\beta_d$ is well recovered in each bin and is remarkably stable across both multipoles and masks. EE values cluster around $\beta_d^{EE} \approx 1.5$ to $1.6$ across individual bins, scattering around the canonical $1.53$ with no systematic trend with $\ell_{\rm eff}$. Most bins are consistent with the global EE estimates ($\sim 1.53$, Section~\ref{sec:dust_pl}) within $1$-$2\sigma$. The BB estimates are even more stable, clustering around $\beta_d^{BB} \approx 1.5$, consistent with the global values of $\sim1.53$. The tight consistency across bins and masks confirms that the modified-blackbody approximation with fixed $T_d = 19.6\,\mathrm{K}$ provides a valid description of the dust SED over the full multipole range $20 \le \ell \le 200$, in agreement with \citet{Planck_2018XI} and \citet{Planck_XXII}.

\subsubsection{Synchrotron-dust spatial correlation $\rho$}

The per-bin $\rho$ posteriors are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:rho_bin-to-bin}. For EE, the most constrained estimates are obtained at low $\ell$, where the values are small and positive, generally in the range $\rho^{EE} \sim 0.09$-$0.10$ depending on the mask, consistent with the trend of decreasing correlation with increasing Galactic latitude cut found in the global fit (Section~\ref{sec:rho_pl}). At higher multipoles the EE estimates become noisier, but remain broadly consistent with small positive values at the $\sim 10\%$ level, in agreement with \citet{Planck_2018XI}.


\paragraph{}

For BB, the per-bin estimates are noisier, especially at small scale bins, where the noise dominates the data. Notably, a handful of high-$\ell$ BB bins yield small negative values, but none exceed $1.5\sigma$ significance. Al larger scales ($\ell \leq 130$), the values scatter around $\sim 0.1$ at all multipoles, consistent with the stable $\rho^{BB} \sim 0.11$-$0.12$ found in the global fit. No significant trend with $\ell_{\rm eff}$ is present in either mode. The overall results are consistent with the findings of \citet{Choi_2015}, who reported per-bin values of $\rho \sim 0.2$ from WMAP 9-year data, and with \citet{Krachmalnicoff_2018}, who found values clustering around $0.1$-$0.2$ across most bins and sky fractions. Our results confirm that the synchrotron-dust spatial correlation is a real but weak signal at the level of $\rho \sim 0.1$-$0.2$, most prominent in EE at large angular scales and in the most inclusive sky fractions.

\paragraph{}

In summary, the bin-to-bin analysis confirms the parameter values obtained in the global power-law fit and provides no evidence for significant scale-dependent deviations in any of the foreground parameters over the multipole range $20 \leq \ell \leq 200$. The spectral indices $\beta_s$ and $\beta_d$ are consistent with their global values in all well-constrained bins, the synchrotron-dust correlation $\rho$ remains small and positive, and the amplitude ratios are stable with $\ell$. Together, these results support the validity of the global power-law parametrization adopted in Section~\ref{sec:results_pl} as an adequate description of the foreground emission over the sky fractions and multipole range considered in this work.




\subsection{Goodness of fit}

The reduced $\chi^2$ values for each bin and mask are listed in the respective bin-to-bin tables. For both EE and BB modes, most bins yield $\chi^2_{\rm red} \approx 1.0$-$1.6$ across all masks, indicating a good agreement between the data and the per-bin model. This should be contrasted with the global power-law fit (Table~\ref{tab:chi2_all}, Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_3}), which produces substantially larger values in most cases. For the QJ+WP+Pl combination over $20 \leq \ell \leq 200$, the EE reduced $\chi^2$ reaches $7.90$, $6.85$, and $2.85$ for the $|b| > 10^\circ$, $15^\circ$, and $20^\circ$ masks, respectively, while the BB values are $2.50$, $1.84$, and $1.60$. The improvement in the bin-to-bin case confirms that the per-bin model, which imposes no global $\ell$-dependence, is able to describe the data at each individual scale, whereas the single power-law model is unable to fully account for the multipole structure of the synchrotron emission over the full range $20 \leq \ell \leq 200$ (see Section~\ref{sec:synch_alp_pl}).

The power-law $\chi^2_{\rm red}$ values improve noticeably both with more restrictive masking and when the first multipole bin is excluded. Restricting the fit to $30 \leq \ell \leq 200$ reduces the EE values to $3.27$, $1.95$, and $1.69$ for the three masks, approaching acceptability. The BB $\chi^2_{\rm red}$ values remain similar across the two multipole intervals, although the posterior parameter constraints differ significantly between these fitting ranges. 

This shows that the poor EE goodness of fit in the baseline analysis is largely driven by the tension introduced by the $\ell_{\rm eff} = 29$ bin, which carries significant constraining power but may not be well described by the same power law that fits the higher-$\ell$ bins. 

The bin-to-bin $\chi^2_{\rm red}$ also tends to decrease with more restrictive masking, although this trend must be interpreted cautiously, as at high Galactic latitudes the data are noisier, which widens the per-bin uncertainties and therefore lowers the $\chi^2_{\rm red}$ rather than reflecting a better model description.
