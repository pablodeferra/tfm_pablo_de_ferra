\section{RESULTS}
\label{sec:results}

In this section we present the results of the foreground power spectrum analysis described in Section~\ref{sec:methodology}. The analysis is performed over the multipole range $20 \leq \ell \leq 199$ using the combined 14-band of QUIJOTE, WMAP and Planck dataset. Two complementary fitting approaches are applied: a global power-law fit (Section~\ref{sec:fitting_methods_pl}) and an independent bin-to-bin fit (Section~\ref{sec:fitting_methods_btb}). For each approach, results are obtained for three Galactic masks defined by latitude cuts at $|b| > 10^\circ$, $15^\circ$, and $20^\circ$ (Section~\ref{sec:masks}), allowing us to assess the robustness of the derived foreground parameters with respect to Galactic plane contamination. In all cases, the EE and BB polarization modes are fitted separately.

\paragraph{}

The foreground model and the Bayesian inference framework are described in Sections~\ref{sec:foreground_model} and \ref{sec:mcmc}, respectively. This analysis follows approaches similar to those of \citet{Planck_2018XI}, \citet{Choi_2015}, \citet{Hensley_2022}, and \citet{Martire_2022}. The baseline results presented here include both auto- and cross-spectra (105 spectra in total). A complementary analysis restricted to cross-spectra only, which avoids any noise bias contribution from the auto-spectra, is presented in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_1}. 

\paragraph{}

The multipole range adopted here starts at $\ell = 20$, rather than the $\ell \geq 30$ range used in several comparable studies \citep{QUIJOTE_IV, Martire_2022}. The choice is motivated by the behaviour of the BB-mode power-law fit: the BB signal is intrinsically fainter than EE and falls steeply with multipole, so the first bin ($\ell_{\rm eff} = 29$) carries a large fraction of the constraining power on the angular spectral index $\alpha_s$. When this bin is excluded, the remaining bins at smaller angular scales provide insufficient leverage to anchor the power-law slope, leading to steeper and poorly constrained values of $\alpha_s$. Including the first bin resolves this degeneracy and yields well-constrained, physically consistent results for the BB mode. This effect is demonstrated in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_2}, where results obtained over the range $30 \leq \ell \leq 199$ are presented for comparison.

\subsection{Power-law fitting}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

The global power-law model (Section~\ref{sec:fitting_methods_pl}) is fitted simultaneously over all nine multipole bins and all 105 frequency spectra. \subsection{Bin-to-bin fitting}
% ---------------------------------------------------------------

In the bin-to-bin approach (Section~\ref{sec:fitting_methods_btb}), each of the nine multipole bins is fitted independently, without imposing any global power-law dependence on $\ell$. The per-bin parameter vector is $\boldsymbol{\theta}_{\rm bin} = \{A_s, \beta_s, A_d, \beta_d, \rho\}$, and results are obtained separately for EE and BB modes. This approach allows the identification of scale-dependent trends and potential deviations from the global power-law behaviour found in Section~\ref{sec:results}. In multipole bins where the signal-to-noise ratio in the low-frequency channels is insufficient to independently constrain the synchrotron SED, the Gaussian prior on $\beta_s$ described in Section~\ref{sec:mcmc} is applied to regularize the fit.

Posterior constraints (median and $68\%$ credible intervals) on $A_s$, $\beta_s$, $A_d$, $\beta_d$, and $\rho$ are reported as a function of the effective multipole $\ell_{\rm eff}$ for each bin. Results are presented for the three Galactic masks below. The corresponding cross-spectra-only results are provided in Appendix~\ref{sec:appendix_1}.
